# Argument — claims per section (step 2)

Reader: a hiring manager, collaborator or client in the US/EU evaluating an AI engineer who is
honest about what is proven. What they already know: nothing — they arrived from GitHub,
LinkedIn or a search. After one screen they should believe: *this engineer ships real systems
and tells the truth about what each one proves.* Proof: repos they can open, case studies with
explicit boundaries, a dated timeline. Next step: open a case study, then book a 15-minute call.

The page as claims, one per section, each with its proof (copy.md §1). The order of the
argument is the order of the page:

1. **Hero** — I am Cristian Cardona: a full-stack and cloud engineer now building AI systems,
   and every claim on this site points at evidence. → the name and role in large type, with the
   evidence rule stated once, plainly.
2. **Work** — Three public reproductions exist, and each states what it demonstrates and what it
   does not. → the three repos and their case studies, boundaries shown, not hidden in
   small print.
3. **Career** — 7+ years of production software across five companies, almost all of it on
   Azure. → the dated timeline from `experience.json`, stacks named.
4. **Evolution** — The move from full-stack to AI engineering is recorded as dated milestones,
   each linked to commits. → the three-phase timeline (foundation / transition / frontier).
5. **Evidence** — Every capability claim on this page points at something you can open and
   inspect. → the evidence records with their honest statuses (developing / demonstrated).
6. **Contact** — Open to conversations about AI engineering, automation and cloud work. →
   email address and one 15-minute intro call (Cal.com) as the primary action.
7. **Footer** — The close: name returns at full width, credits, colophon, bilingual notice. →
   designed close, not a sitemap.

No section was deleted: all seven carry a claim and its proof.
